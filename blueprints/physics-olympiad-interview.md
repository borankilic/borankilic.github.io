# Physics Olympiad post — interview questions

Answer inline under each question. Rough notes are fine — fragments, half-sentences, the wrong word in
the right place. Voice matters more than polish here; the writing pass can tidy prose but cannot invent
your memories. Skip anything that doesn't land.

**Length labels:** `[1 sentence]` — just the fact. `[short]` — 2–4 sentences. `[open]` — as long as you want.

---

## C. The questions themselves (you flagged this — the contrast with school)

**6.** What actually makes an olympiad problem different from a textbook or exam problem? Try to be
concrete — is it that the path isn't signposted, that it combines topics that are taught separately,
that there's no worked example resembling it, that it needs an idea rather than a method? `[open]`

>

**7.** Can you describe one specific problem well enough to put in the post — the setup, what made it
hard, and the turn that cracked it? It doesn't need to be famous or hard by olympiad standards, just
one you remember clearly. `[open]`

>

**8.** School physics rewards recognising which formula applies. What does olympiad physics reward
instead? `[short]`

>

## D. The struggle (you flagged: days on a single question)

**9.** What was the longest you stayed with one problem, and what did those days actually look like?
Did you carry it around between other things, or sit at a desk with it? `[open]`

>

**10.** What did you do when you were properly stuck — what were your moves? Change representation,
simplify to a special case, work backwards, drop it and return, look for an invariant, something else?
`[open]`

>

**11.** You said it taught you to be stubborn and to look for alternative routes. Was there a point
where stubbornness stopped being useful — where the right call was to abandon an approach? How did you
learn to tell those apart? `[short]`

>

**12.** Did you ever not solve one, after days? What did that feel like, and what did you take from it?
`[short]`

## F. The payoff (you flagged: joy of understanding, feeling of triumph)

**15.** You described the joy of thoroughly understanding every aspect of a system. What does that state
feel like from the inside — what changes when you go from "I can get the answer" to "I understand this
completely"? `[open]`

>

**16.** And the triumph after finally solving one — how would you describe it to someone who's never had
it? Is it relief, or something more like elation? Does it last? `[short]`

>

**17.** Is that feeling the reason you kept going, or was it something else? `[short]`
