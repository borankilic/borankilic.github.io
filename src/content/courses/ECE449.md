Cross-listed as CS 446, this is UIUC's core machine-learning course: linear models, SVMs and kernels, decision trees, neural networks and backpropagation, clustering and mixture models, and the probabilistic and optimisation foundations underneath them. Taken on exchange at UIUC.

What stuck with me most was the first machine problem: building **`mini_torch`**, a small PyTorch-style autograd library in NumPy alone. It meant implementing `Tensor` objects that record a dynamic computation graph during the forward pass, then walking that graph in reverse for `backward()` — deriving and coding the forward and backward pass of every operation, a `Linear` layer, activations, an MSE loss, and an SGD optimiser, and training small networks with the familiar `zero_grad()` / `loss.backward()` / `step()` loop. Writing automatic differentiation myself, rather than calling it, is what finally made frameworks like PyTorch feel legible instead of magical — and it's a good reminder that most "black boxes" are just careful bookkeeping underneath. Homework solutions are below; project code is on my [GitHub](https://github.com/borankilic).
